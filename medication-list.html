current, printable, and always in a pocket

The single most useful document in an emergency, and the one families most often recite from memory at the worst possible moment. Build it once, keep it current, and print a copy for the bag, the school, the respite worker, and the glovebox.

In an emergency

Hand this list over rather than reciting it. Say allergies first, then anything taken today, then the rest. If a dose has changed recently, say so — it is the question that most often gets missed and most often matters.

Reading this as a professional

Families keep this because reconciliation lists are frequently wrong, and because nobody else holds supplements, creams, as-needed medication and the practical detail of how something is actually taken in one place.

The "how it gets taken" field is the one worth reading. A medication that is prescribed and not tolerated in its prescribed form is a medication not being given, and that is often invisible from the chart.

Nothing here is medical advice, and this is not a medical record. It is a family's own working list. Verify doses against the prescription label and your prescriber, and never change a dose based on anything written here.
