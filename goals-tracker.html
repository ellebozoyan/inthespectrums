Goals and generalization:
one page every provider can see

Most children with several providers have several sets of goals that nobody else has read. This puts them in one place, then does the part that actually changes outcomes — naming which goals should be practiced in other settings, and giving whoever is there the specifics they need to do it well.

This stays on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere, there is no account, and nobody, including us, can see what you write. It is saved in the browser on the device that you are currently using, so it is still here tomorrow, on that device, in that browser, and nowhere else.

Which goals should travel?

The part that changes outcomes. A skill practiced in one room for one hour a week rarely turns into a skill a person actually has. Pick the goals worth carrying into the rest of the day, and say precisely how.

Keep it to three per provider. More than that and nothing gets done properly. The whole point is that a small number of things happen everywhere, consistently, not that everyone tries to do everything.

More than one person

Why this matters more than the goals themselves. Generalization is the hardest problem in therapy — a skill that lives only in a clinic room is not yet a skill. Naming a small number of goals that travel, and telling everyone precisely how, is the single highest-return thing a family can organize.

The choosing behavior support page covers who should be setting these targets and who carries them: direction flows from the specialist who owns the domain, and everyone else extends it into the day.

Reading this as a professional

Families are the only people who see every plan. This exists because nobody else is positioned to notice that four disciplines have written overlapping goals, or that a skill you established two months ago has never been asked for anywhere else.

If you are asked to add goals here: paste the exact wording from your plan, keep generalization targets to about three, and be specific enough that an untrained adult could carry it out — what, when, where, with whom, how to prompt, and how to fade. Vagueness is what makes carryover fail.
