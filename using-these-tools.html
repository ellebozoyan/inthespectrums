Using these tools:
saving, printing, backing up

The trackers on this site are single web pages that run entirely on your own device. That makes them private and durable — and it means a few practical things are useful to know, because nobody is holding a copy for you.

We cannot offer technical support

This site is written and maintained by one person, for free, and there is no help desk behind it. These instructions are offered in good faith and were accurate when written; devices and operating systems change constantly, and yours may look different.

If something doesn't work on your device, the honest advice is to search for how to do it on your specific phone or computer — that will get you a better answer than we can. And you are welcome to take any of these tools, change them, or host them yourself.

Some people will find these useful as they are. Others will use them mainly as a list of what is worth tracking, then build something of their own in a notebook, a spreadsheet, or an app they already like. Both are good outcomes.

How they actually work

  • Nothing you type ever leaves your device. There is no account, no server, and no way for us or anyone else to see what you write.
  • The trackers remember; the template builders don't. Trackers save to this browser so your entries are there tomorrow. The document builders clear when you close them, by design.
  • The data lives in one browser on one device — the device that you are currently using. Not in your account, not in the cloud. Safari on your phone and Chrome on your laptop are two separate, unconnected copies.
  • Clearing your browsing data deletes it. So does a private or incognito window, which can't save anything at all.
  • The backup file is the only insurance. Download one every few weeks and keep it somewhere real.

Adding it to your home screen

Worth doing. It opens like an app, full screen, one tap — which is the difference between logging something in twenty seconds and not logging it at all.

iPhone and iPad

Open the tracker in Safari — this doesn't work from Chrome on iOS.

  • Tap the Share button (the square with the arrow)
  • Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen
  • Name it something short, then tap Add

The tellThe saved data belongs to Safari. If you later open the same page in another browser, it will look empty — that's expected, not lost.

Android

Open the tracker in Chrome.

  • Tap the three dots menu, top right
  • Tap Add to Home screen or Install app
  • Confirm the name and tap Add

Worth knowingSamsung Internet and other browsers have the same feature in a similar place, but each keeps its own separate copy of your data.

Mac

Bookmark it, or make it a dock app.

  • Safari: File → Add to Dock, in recent versions
  • Chrome: three dots → Cast, Save and Share → Create Shortcut
  • Otherwise just bookmark it and pin the tab

What mattersWhichever browser you use, keep using that one. Each holds its own separate data.

Windows

Chrome and Edge both install web pages as apps.

  • Chrome: three dots → Cast, Save and Share → Install page as app
  • Edge: three dots → Apps → Install this site as an app
  • Otherwise bookmark it and pin the tab

The pointAn installed app and the same page in a tab share the same stored data, as long as it's the same browser.

Saving a summary as a PDF

Every tool has a print button. On every modern device, "print" also means "save as PDF" — which is usually what you actually want, so you can email it or keep it.

iPhone and iPad

Tap the print button on the page, then in the print preview, pinch outward on the page image or tap the share icon and choose Save to Files.

TipYou can also share it straight to Mail or Messages from that screen.

Android

Tap print, then in the printer dropdown at the top choose Save as PDF, and tap the download button.

TipIt usually lands in your Downloads folder or Google Drive, depending on what you pick.

Mac

Press Cmd + P or use the print button, then in the print dialog use the PDF dropdown at the bottom left and choose Save as PDF.

TipTurn off headers and footers in the print options for a cleaner page.

Windows

Press Ctrl + P or use the print button, then choose Microsoft Print to PDF or Save as PDF as the printer.

TipIn Chrome, More settings → uncheck Headers and footers.

Backing up, and moving to another device

There is no sync, because there is no server. Moving your data means moving a file — which is more work, and also why nobody else can ever see it.

Backing up

  • Open any tracker, go to the export or setup tab, and choose Download a backup file.
  • One file covers every tool on this site and every person you've added.
  • Put it somewhere that isn't the device it came from — cloud storage, email it to yourself, a USB drive.
  • Do it monthly, and before any phone upgrade, browser change, or "clear my browsing data."

Restoring, or moving to a new device

  • Open the tracker on the new device or browser.
  • Choose Restore from a backup and select the file.
  • It replaces whatever is currently saved there, so restore onto an empty one where you can.

If two people are both logging

There is no merge. If you and a partner both log on separate phones, you will end up with two separate records, and restoring one over the other will overwrite it.

The practical options are: pick one device as the real one and have the other person tell you what to add; use a shared tablet that lives in the kitchen; or accept two records and combine them by hand when you print. Not elegant, and it's the honest cost of nothing being stored on a server.

If something goes wrong

  • Everything disappeared. Most often you've opened it in a different browser, or in a private window, or browsing data was cleared. Check the other browser first, then restore from your backup.
  • It says it can't save. You're probably in a private or incognito window. Open a normal one.
  • The print looks wrong. Turn off headers and footers in the print settings, and try landscape for wide tables.
  • The page looks broken. Refresh. If it persists, the site may have updated while your browser cached an old version — a hard refresh usually fixes it.
  • Nothing on this list helped. Search for your exact device and the thing you're trying to do. We can't troubleshoot individual devices, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

Several people, one set of tools

How it works if you are keeping records for more than one person.

  • Everything is shared between the tools. A name entered once appears in all of them. So does the age, the diagnoses, and the emergency information — you do not enter any of it twice.
  • Each person keeps their own separate records. Medications, foods, goals, calendars and everything else stay with the person they belong to.
  • Switch with the bar at the top of every tool. It says who you are working on, and one click moves you to somebody else or adds a new person.
  • Once there are more than a handful of people, a search box appears — start typing a name and the list narrows.
  • The backup file covers everybody. One download, every person, every tool. One restore brings it all back.

If you are a professional thinking of using this for a caseload

The tools will technically hold many people, and some clinicians and teachers will want to use them that way. Before you do, a few things worth weighing honestly — because this was designed for families, and that shows.

  • It is stored in one browser, on one device, unencrypted. No password, no login, no lock. Anybody who opens that browser can read all of it.
  • On a shared or school computer, that is a real problem. So is a lost laptop.
  • There is no audit trail — no record of who viewed or changed anything, which most professional record-keeping requires.
  • Clearing browsing data deletes everything, with no server copy and no recovery.
  • Records about other people’s children carry obligations — educational and health privacy rules, retention requirements, and your employer’s policies. This site cannot help you meet them.

What we would suggest instead: use these tools with a family, on their device, as a way of building something they own and take away. Or use them to prepare a printed document for a session, and keep your actual records in whatever system your organization requires.

Two or three children in one household is what this was built for, and it does that well. A caseload of thirty is a different job with different obligations.

Before you close this
