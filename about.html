About, and the people who built this

This site exists because the information families need is scattered across a dozen systems that don't talk to each other. It was assembled by people who work inside those systems, and it's given away because it should never have been hard to find.

Who writes this

Founder and author

Elle Bozoyan

Integrative health coach · curriculum developer · author

Elle is an integrative health coach trained at the Institute for Integrative Nutrition, with additional culinary training and more than two decades of teaching movement — Pilates, yoga, barre, spin, and outdoor adventure guiding, including wilderness leadership and risk management coursework that shapes the safety material on this site.

She develops social and emotional learning curriculum for children, and is the author of the Maplewood Project — a fifty-two book picture book series, a play schema library, and a growing set of companion tools for families, classrooms, and clinics.

In The Spectrums began as a set of documents built to solve specific problems: how to explain a child to a new teacher, how to survive an evaluation, how to get a claim paid. It turned out that everyone needed them.

What you should know about this list

This is not a directory, a vetting service, or an endorsement of outcomes. These are practitioners whose thinking informed pages on this site, listed because families kept asking where to find people who work this way.

In The Spectrums receives no payment, commission, or referral fee from anyone listed here, and no one has paid to appear. If that ever changes, it will be stated on this page before the listing goes up. Fees, availability, licensure, and whether someone can practice in your state are entirely between you and them — and licensure does limit who can work with you across state lines, so ask early.

Before adding anyone here

  • Get written permission, and have each person write or approve their own entry. A practitioner's name on a public site is their professional reputation, not yours to place.
  • Confirm the credentials as they want them stated, including current licensure and which states they can practice in.
  • Agree what happens if they stop taking clients, or want to come off the list. Give them a way to ask, and honor it quickly.
  • Keep the list small. Three practitioners you'd send a friend to is worth more than thirty, and a short list is easier to keep current.
  • Review it annually with the rest of the site.

Everyone who said this part is wrong

Several pages exist in their current form because someone read a draft and pushed back. That's the most useful thing anyone does here.

Tell us what's missing or wrong

Every page on this site is a draft that hasn't been corrected yet. Program names change, codes change, guidance changes, and some of it was probably never quite right.

If you find something out of date, something that doesn't match your state, or something that reads as certain when it shouldn't — say so. Corrections are the most valuable thing anyone sends.

The same goes if a page is missing. Much of what's here exists because someone asked a question nobody had written down the answer to.

Using this material

Take it. Print it, adapt it, translate it, put it in a folder for your school team, hand it to a family at a clinic. Change the wording so it sounds like you — you know your people, and your voice is what will carry it. Attribution is appreciated and not required. The point is that it gets used and that it helps.

The best resource is the one that arrives before you knew you needed it.
