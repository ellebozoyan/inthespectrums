The Kitchen STEAM
food journal

The child's book, not the school's. Built so that a child who never tastes a thing can fill in every page of it, and built so that it cannot quietly turn into a record of who ate what. Print it, or use your browser's print command on this page. It belongs with the course guide and the forty-six cards.

The short version. The scales in here ask how crunchy and how strong, not how much you liked it. There is no page that accumulates across weeks into a progress chart, because that is the shape a journal takes just before it becomes evidence. The liking question exists, it is small, it is optional, and a child can answer it about how something looked.

Before you print it

Five rules for the adult. They are the reason the journal is built the way it is.

What this book is never used for

Never collect it, grade it, compare it, or display it. The moment a journal is assessed it becomes a compliance document. If you want to see a page, ask the child to show you, and accept no.

Never record quantity eaten or number of new foods tried. Intake logging is contraindicated for children with eating disorders, and it offers nothing that sensory recording does not offer better.

Never send a food diary home. Asking a child to write down what they ate at home exposes household food insecurity to the class, and it teaches that food is something to be audited.

Never make a wall display of who tried what. Class-level anonymous results are excellent. Individual tallies are not.

Never carry a rating forward as evidence. A rating is one child's impression on one day. If it changes over a term, that is theirs to notice.

Print the front matter and one session spread per session. Print the extra pages only as a card calls for them, or leave a stack on a side table so anybody can take one. Single-sided works better than double, because several pages are drawn on heavily.

Page 2

This journal is mine

Which means a few things are true about it.

  • Nobody marks it and nobody collects it. If a grown-up wants to see a page they have to ask, and you can say no. It lives in your bag and it goes home with you.
  • There are no wrong answers in here.
  • You never have to taste anything. Ever. Looking, listening, smelling, cooking and describing all count, and you can fill in every page of this journal without tasting a single thing.
  • “Not for me today” is a whole sentence. You do not have to explain it.
  • Nobody comments on anybody else's plate. That is the rule for everyone, including the grown-ups.
  • You can change your mind. About a food, about a word, about anything you wrote last week.

Why the intensity scales come before the faces. How crunchy and how strong a smell are questions with no right answer and no virtue in a high score, so every child can answer them fully and nobody's answer is better than anybody else's. The liking question sits underneath, deliberately smaller, and a child can answer it about a smell rather than a taste. Reverse that order and the page becomes a preference record.

Extra pages, printed as you need them

Not part of every child's booklet. Print the ones a session calls for, or leave a stack on the side table so anybody can take one.

One thing, looked at properly

Look at it for two whole minutes before you draw anything. Time it. Then draw it big enough to fill the box, note how big it really is, what you noticed in minute two, and three things a photograph would miss.

Card A04
What's inside

The outside on the left, the cut face on the right. Label lines that must not cross each other, and a line showing how long an inch or a centimeter is.

Cards A04 and A08
Pattern

Print, stamp or draw straight onto the page. Find the one small piece the whole pattern is made from, and draw it in the little box. Then find the mistake, because every hand-made pattern has one.

Cards A03 and A08
An experiment

The first two boxes are filled in before anything happens. A prediction written down beforehand is worth more than a right answer afterward. Then the one thing changed, the things kept the same, what happened, and what you would test next.

Any science card
Somebody's story

Questions that cannot be answered with yes or no, written before you ask them. Then stop talking and wait three whole seconds after they finish, because people almost always add the best part in the pause. Includes a box for whether they said it could be shared.

Cards T02 and C01
Six pictures, in order

The six moments that tell the whole story, with what happens and what we hear underneath. Done before anything is filmed.

Card T01
What shall we call it?

The same dish named three ways: plain, describing, and with the story in it. Then the favorite name written three times in three kinds of lettering, and everything that goes in it. A name that hides an ingredient is not a clever name.

Card A10
Three cups, one difference

What we were comparing, what my job in the test was, how many slips came back, how many were right, and how many would have been right by guessing. The numbers belong to the class, and nobody writes down who tasted.

Card N04
Count the spirals

Mark one spiral all the way around with stickers so you do not lose your place, then count how many run each way. Draw the specimen, add the class's numbers, and work out the rule that makes the next number.

Card M01
A score for kitchen sounds

Invent a shape for each sound. Long sounds get long marks, high sounds go high up, loud sounds get big ones, and time runs left to right. Somebody else has to be able to play it.

Card A09
Design a dish

Plan it on paper before you touch anything. Where should the eye land first, and what is your rule for this plate? Odd numbers, off center, one line, a third of it empty.

Card A05
Rules at my table

Every family has rules nobody ever said out loud. Write three of yours. There are no correct ones, which is what makes comparing them interesting. Then a rule from somebody else's table that you liked.

Cards N05 and C06
Who fed me

Somebody grew it, somebody moved it, somebody cooked it. Most of them you will never meet. Some of them are in this building. Ends with a note you can tear out and actually give to one of them.

Cards C04 and C05
Squared paper, for whatever you need

A graph, a floor plan, a pattern, a layer diagram, a map of the kitchen.

Before you close this

If you change one thing in this journal, keep the intensity scales above the faces and keep the eight ways unnumbered. Those two decisions are what stop a workbook from turning into a record of which children eat.

And if a child fills the drawing boxes and leaves every word box empty all term, the journal is working. Drawing what something looks like is describing it.
