The meal planner:
your week, your call

Plan a week of meals from foods that already work — and, if you want to, put something new in it. You decide whether to try anything new and how often. Nobody else sets that number, and choosing zero this week is a real and reasonable answer.

Who this is for

Built so that the person eating can do this themselves, with an adult or without one. Picking your own meals is one of the most ordinary kinds of control there is, and it is often one of the first pieces of a day someone can genuinely own.

It uses whatever is on the food list, so add foods there first. Nothing is sent anywhere.

This stays on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere, there is no account, and nobody — including us — can see what you write. It saves in the browser on the device that you are currently using, so it is here tomorrow.

That also means it lives in one browser on one device. If you clear your browsing data, use a different phone, or lose the device, it is gone. Download a backup file every few weeks — see using these tools.

How many new things this week?

Entirely your choice, and you can change it whenever you like. There is no right number, and zero is a real answer — some weeks are for keeping things steady, and that is its own kind of sensible.

Choose something

A plan is not a promise. If a new food is on the plan and the day arrives and it is not going to happen, take it off. Nothing is lost — planning to try something is itself a step, and it was never a contract.

The feeding therapy page covers why pressure works against eating, and why a person choosing their own food is doing something that matters more than what ends up on the plate.

Reading this as a professional

The goal setting is deliberately handed to the person. A target chosen by the eater is a different thing psychologically from one set by an adult, and it is the version far more likely to survive contact with an actual mealtime.

Where a family shows you a week with zero new foods, that is information rather than a problem — it usually means capacity is low somewhere else, and it is worth asking where.

Nothing here is medical or nutritional advice. Restricted eating can have medical causes and consequences. A shrinking list or a nutritionally narrow one warrants review by a clinician who knows the person.
