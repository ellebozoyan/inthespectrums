The practice mirror:
see yourself inside the movement

A therapist films the movement they want. At home, that clip plays see-through over the live camera, so the person can line themselves up inside it while they move. Record an attempt, compare the two side by side in slow motion, and send it back before the next session.

What this does and does not do

It does not measure anything. There is no motion tracking, no accuracy score, no automatic analysis. Those exist in commercial sports apps and they need servers and machine learning; this is a mirror with a target laid over it, and it is honest about being that.

Nothing is uploaded, ever. No account, no server, no cloud. Video never leaves your device unless you deliberately save a file and send it yourself, the way you already send anything else.

Which means sharing is manual. Save the clip, then email it or attach it in whatever portal your clinic uses. Slower than automatic — and it means nobody is holding videos of your child but you.

About the clips

Model clips are kept in the browser's own storage on the device that you are currently using, which is limited. Keep them short — five to ten seconds is plenty — and expect to hold a handful rather than a library.

Attempts are never saved. They exist while the tab is open, and disappear when you close it, unless you save the file yourself.

Your exercises

The practice log

What was done and when. Print this for the next session — it is often more useful than any single video.

Why the overlay works. Copying a movement from memory is hard; copying one you can see yourself inside is much easier, and it removes the need for anybody to describe the movement in words. For a person who processes visual information differently, or who finds verbal instruction hard to follow, that difference is not small.

See physical therapy, occupational therapy, myofunctional therapy and speech and language for what tends to be practised between sessions.

Reading this as a professional

If you film a model clip for a family, film it from the angle you want them to copy, keep it to a few seconds, and say the cues out loud even though the audio may be muted here — families replay these with sound on.

There is no measurement in this tool and it should not be described to a family as providing one. What it provides is a visual target and a practice record. The log is the part worth reviewing in session.

Nothing here is clinical advice, and this does not measure anything. Exercises should come from a clinician who has assessed the person. Stop anything that causes pain.

Video never leaves your device. Sharing is something you do deliberately, by saving a file and sending it yourself.
