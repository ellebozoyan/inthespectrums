The food list:
what is accepted, and exactly how

For a person with a very short list of accepted foods, that list is not a preference — it is access to eating at all. This keeps it written down: what works, the brand, the plate, the temperature, the way it is cut. So that a hospital, a school, a grandparent or a new therapist can get it right the first time.

This stays on your device. Nothing sent anywhere, no account. It saves in the browser on the device that you are currently using, so it is here tomorrow — on that device, in that browser, and nowhere else. Download a backup every few weeks — see using these tools.

Add a food

Target foods appear as options in the meal planner, so trying something new is always a choice rather than a surprise.

Target means a food you are hoping to get to one day — not being worked on yet. Being explored means it is actively in play. Keeping those apart matters, because a target list can be long and hopeful while the exploring list stays very short.

Be specific to the point of feeling silly. The brand matters. The shape matters. Somebody who has never met this person will read it.

Who this is for

Shared with the other tools on this site.

Protect the accepted list first. Everything on the feeding therapy page rests on that one idea. Expansion is slow and it is real, but it happens from a stable base — and a list that keeps shrinking is the thing to raise with a clinician, promptly.

Reading this as a professional

If a family hands you this, the specificity is not rigidity — it is the conditions under which eating happens at all. Brand and preparation detail are access requirements rather than preferences, and substituting is the most common way an admission or a placement stops a person eating.

The "what changed" record is the part worth reading first. A shrinking list over months is a clinical finding.

Nothing here is medical or nutritional advice. Restricted eating can have medical causes and consequences, and a shrinking list warrants review by a clinician who knows the person.
