What to say:
the sentences, and where they live

Most of this site is about understanding what is going on. This page is for the other moment, the one at seven in the evening when something is happening and the useful thing is not an explanation but a sentence. It collects the wording already scattered across the site and sorts it by the situation you are actually in.

These are examples, not correct answers. Every one of them was written for a particular kind of moment and a particular person, and yours is different. Take the shape, change the words, say it the way you talk. A sentence delivered awkwardly in your own voice does more than a polished one that does not sound like you.

Sentences appear on the pages themselves in their own color, so you can find them by skimming rather than by reading.

Communication and AAC

Three things that decide whether any of this works

Length, before anything else. A person at capacity cannot process a careful three-clause sentence, however kind it is. Four words delivered beat a good sentence delivered too late, and this is where the well-intentioned version most often fails.

Whether the question is real. Asking ready? and then starting regardless teaches faster than not asking at all, because it establishes that the question is decoration. Where the answer cannot be no, say what is about to happen instead of asking.

Whether it is repeatable. The sentence that works is the one every adult in the house can say the same way on a bad Tuesday. A beautiful sentence only one person can deliver is not a plan.

Where these came from

Nothing on this page is new. Every sentence lives on the page that explains why it works, because a script without its reasoning becomes an instruction, and an instruction that does not fit your child gets used anyway and then fails, and you conclude you were the problem.

So this page routes rather than collects. Follow whichever line sounds like your week, read the few paragraphs around it, and take the wording from there.

Before you close this

You already say most of this. Nobody arrives at a page like this without a long history of finding words in difficult moments, usually without help and usually while tired.

What a collection like this is actually for is the handful you have not needed yet, and the ones you would rather not invent for the first time while it is happening. Read one section. Leave the rest until the week you need it.

Nothing here is medical, legal or educational advice. These are examples of wording, gathered from across this site, offered so you have somewhere to start rather than a blank moment.
