The symptom tracker:
write down what changed, not everything

Most trackers fail because they ask for too much. This one asks what was different today. Log in twenty seconds, then print a clean summary the day before an appointment — so the answer to "how has he been?" isn't whatever you can remember while a child climbs the exam table.

This stays on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere, there is no account, and nobody — including us — can see what you write. It's saved in this browser so it's still here tomorrow.

That also means it lives in one browser on one device. If you clear your browsing data, use a different phone, or lose the device, it's gone. Download a backup file from the Print & export tab every few weeks and keep it somewhere real. Using these tools explains how, and how to add this to your home screen.

What was different?

Tap an area, then tap what you saw. Severity and notes are optional. If nothing stood out, log nothing — a blank day is data too, and it's what "better" looks like on a chart.

How full was the bottle?

Capacity is finite and it fills up. Demands, transitions, noise, disappointments, and the effort of holding it together all go in — and a hard moment is usually a stacking that finally went past capacity, not a response to whatever happened last. One tap, and over time this will explain more than any single symptom.

Early signs, before it got hard

The most valuable thing on this page. Precursors are specific to a person, they appear earlier than most people notice, and a written list of them is what lets a new teacher or carer help before anyone has to shout. Tap whatever you saw first — the tracker builds the list for you.

What helped?

Usually left out of trackers, and often the most useful thing in them. What actually worked today — for calming, for getting through something, for turning a moment around.

The day around it

The context that makes a symptom mean something later. Sleep and anything that changed are the two that earn their keep.

Restraint, seclusion, or a removal

Only if it happened. Keep your own record alongside whatever the school sends — repeated use is evidence that a support plan is failing, not that a child is difficult, and a parallel log is what makes a pattern impossible to dismiss.

What keeps coming up

Each bar is one day over the last 60. Height is severity; a faint bar means you logged that day but didn't note this one.

Be careful with this part. A tracker shows you what happened alongside what else. It cannot tell you what caused what — two things rising together is the most ordinary coincidence there is, and it is also what every promising theory looks like at the start.

Use it to notice something worth asking about, then bring it to someone who can test it properly. "This got worse in the two weeks after the dose change, here are the days" is a genuinely useful sentence in an appointment. "The supplement caused it" is a conclusion you don't have yet.

Reading this as a professional

This is deliberately exception-based rather than a daily checklist. Families abandon comprehensive trackers within weeks, and the sparse data that results is worse than none because the gaps read as absence rather than as unrecorded. What arrives in your hands is what a parent thought was worth writing down, on the days they had capacity — treat the density of logging as itself informative.

The printed summary leads with frequency and severity over a date range, lists context changes chronologically, reports the precursor and what-helped tallies, and reproduces the parent's own notes verbatim. If you want something different on it, tell the family — it's their document and the wording is theirs to change.

Nothing is stored on a server, so there is no record for you to request and nothing to sign. If a family wants you to have it, they print it or hand you the CSV.

Take this page, change it, host it yourself, put your own name on it. It is one file with nothing behind it — it works offline, and it will still work in ten years when whatever app you were considering has been acquired and shut down.

Nothing here is medical advice, and a tracker is not a diagnosis. If something frightens you, don't wait for the pattern to become clear — call the nurse line. New behavior is a symptom until proven otherwise, and children who cannot say where it hurts depend on somebody else noticing.
