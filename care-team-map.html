Reading this as a clinician or educator

Useful in two directions: knowing which specialty owns a question before you refer, and knowing what to tell a family to expect from the appointment you're sending them to.

The access notes at the end of each entry — waitlists, what to ask for, what gets denied — are the parts families most often need explained by someone they trust.

One last thing

You do not need to understand all forty-five of these, and nobody does.

You need the two or three that match what you are living with right now — and you can find those, which is the entire skill. Come back when the questions change.

This is a map, not medical advice. Nothing here diagnoses anyone or replaces a conversation with your child's own clinicians. Titles, training, and what a given office actually offers vary by region and by practice.

Written in person-first language. If a description here doesn't match your family's experience, trust your experience.
